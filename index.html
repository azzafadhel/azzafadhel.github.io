About Me

I am a Computer Science Ph.D. candidate at Washington State University. I build data-efficient machine learning methods that identify promising designs when data are scarce and experiments are expensive.

My work connects offline optimization, probabilistic modeling, and Bayesian optimization with scientific practice. I have led AI research for nanoporous materials, metal 3D printing, and wearable health systems. In a physical printing deployment, our method found six feasible settings across four laser-power levels after 37 earlier manual trials found none. My research appears at ICML, AISTATS, and AAAI.

I develop research software, evaluate methods against strong baselines, and work with domain scientists to test model recommendations. I am interested in Research Scientist, Applied Scientist, Research Engineer, and ML Engineer opportunities, including internships as I complete my Ph.D.

Research Projects

ICML 2024Offline optimization

Learning Surrogates for Offline Black-Box Optimization via Gradient Matching

Problem

A model trained on a fixed dataset can fit observed values yet give misleading search directions when an optimizer moves beyond the data.

Approach

MATCH-OPT constructs monotonically improving input sequences from offline data and trains a surrogate to align its gradient with the improvement along those sequences.

Main results
0.283Best mean normalized rank for the top candidate across six DesignBench tasks; lower is better.
0.350Best mean normalized rank for the median candidate across the same tasks.

Results from Tables 1 and 2 of the ICML paper.

My role: I led the experimental work: implementation of the trajectory and loss pipelines, surrogate training, candidate search, benchmark comparisons, and empirical analysis.

AAAI 2026Materials discovery

Nanoporous Materials Discovery via Search Bias-Guided Surrogate Modeling

Problem

Large pools of COFs and MOFs contain promising candidates, but property evaluations are costly and historical data alone do not reliably rank unseen materials.

Approach

OptReg adds an optimization-bias regularizer to value prediction, using improving sequences in offline data to train a surrogate for candidate discovery.

Main results
125.8%Maximum improvement over the best training COF for methane storage.
309.1%Maximum improvement over the best training MOF for volumetric hydrogen capacity.

Results use 3,000 training materials and 100 selected candidates; values are improvements over the best training material (Table 1).

My role: I led the ML work from problem formulation and method design through training, candidate discovery, baseline comparisons, analysis, and writing.

AISTATS 2026Small-data optimization

Black-Box Optimization from Small Offline Datasets via Meta Learning with Synthetic Tasks

Problem

When only a tiny, low-valued subset of past observations is available, conventional surrogates struggle to learn where high-value designs lie.

Approach

OptBias generates related synthetic functions with Sim4Opt, meta-learns a reusable optimization bias, and fine-tunes the surrogate on the target dataset.

Main results
1.33Best mean rank across six benchmarks using the lowest-valued 1% of offline data.
4 / 6Benchmarks on which OptBias achieved the highest reported candidate performance.

Results from Table 1 of the AISTATS paper.

My role: I led the project: OptBias and Sim4Opt design, meta-learning implementation, optimization experiments, ablations, and writing.

AAAI 2026Deployed AI

Discovery of Feasible 3D Printing Configurations for Metal Alloys via AI-Driven Adaptive Experimental Design

Problem

Printing GRCop-42 at lower laser powers requires finding feasible settings among more than 100 million combinations for each power level; each lab trial consumes time and materials.

Approach

BEAM combines a probabilistic feasibility model, an active-search acquisition strategy, and expert constraints to guide the next physical printing trials.

Main results
6Feasible configurations discovered across 950 W, 700 W, 600 W, and 500 W.
≤ 10Trials to find a feasible setting at each tested power level.

The lab’s 37 earlier manual trials found no feasible setting. BEAM found three at 600 W and one at each other power level (Table 1).

My role: I led the AI/ML work: formulation, surrogate and acquisition design, planning with materials-science collaborators, experimental analysis, and writing.

AAAI 2027 · Under reviewWearable systems

OptWear: AI-Driven Human-Centered Design of Wearable Health Systems

Problem

Wearable health systems must balance energy and latency while realistic hardware measurements and user evaluations are expensive.

Approach

OptWear uses multi-objective, multi-fidelity search to find promising designs, validates selected designs on hardware, and explores how user feedback can inform later refinement.

Main results
85.2%Mean resource cost saved across five health applications versus single-fidelity search.
0.49%Mean Pareto hypervolume loss in that cost comparison.

The reported cost comparison uses lower and intermediate fidelities; a six-participant pilot collected user feedback. Preference-guided refinement is a future stage.

My role: I led the AI/ML formulation, surrogate modeling, Bayesian optimization, experiments, and analysis in collaboration with the wearable-systems lead.
