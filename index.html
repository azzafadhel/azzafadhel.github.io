About Me

I am a Computer Science Ph.D. candidate at Washington State University, working on machine learning for optimization and decision-making with limited data. I develop methods that help identify promising solutions when collecting data or running experiments is expensive.

My research combines Bayesian optimization, probabilistic machine learning, offline optimization, and generative modeling. I design and develop learning and optimization methods for problems in materials discovery, metal 3D printing, wearable systems, and other scientific and engineering domains. My work has been published at the International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Statistics (AISTATS), and AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI).

My work combines algorithm development with hands-on implementation. I work across the research process, from formulating problems and developing new methods to implementing, deploying, and evaluating them with real-world data and experiments.

I am currently interested in Ph.D. research internships and future full-time opportunities as a Research Scientist, Applied Scientist, or Research Engineer.

Research Projects

International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML) 2024 Offline Optimization

Learning Surrogates for Offline Black-Box Optimization via Gradient Matching

Challenge

When optimization relies only on previously collected data, learned models can become unreliable when searching beyond the designs they have already seen, leading to poor recommendations.

What We Built

We developed MATCH-OPT, which learns both observed outcomes and which directions in the design space lead to better solutions, providing better guidance when searching beyond the available data.

Results
Up to 123%higher normalized objective value than the best design available in the offline dataset.
#1 Overallbest mean normalized rank across six tasks among 10 evaluated methods.

Improved over the best design in the offline data on all 6 benchmark tasks.

My role: Led the experimental implementation and evaluation, including surrogate training, candidate generation, and large-scale benchmark experiments.

AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI) 2026Materials Discovery

Nanoporous Materials Discovery via Search Bias-Guided Surrogate Modeling

Challenge

Searching for high-performing materials is expensive because only a small fraction of candidate designs can be evaluated. Models trained on existing data can predict known materials well but still struggle to identify the best unseen candidates.

What We Built

We developed OptReg, a surrogate-learning method that learns not only to predict material properties, but also which directions in the design space lead toward better candidates, improving how materials are ranked for discovery.

Results
Up to 126%improvement over the best material in the training data for methane storage.
Up to 309%improvement over the best material in the training data for hydrogen storage.

Evaluated on nanoporous-material discovery tasks using 3,000 training materials and a budget of 100 selected candidates.

My role: Led the ML research end-to-end, including problem formulation, method design, implementation, candidate discovery, and evaluation.

International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Statistics (AISTATS) 2026Small-Data Optimization

Black-Box Optimization from Small Offline Datasets via Meta Learning with Synthetic Tasks

Challenge

In many scientific and engineering problems, historical data are not only scarce but also dominated by poor-performing designs, making it difficult to identify better solutions without collecting costly new data.

What We Built

We developed OptBias, a meta-learning framework that learns useful search behavior from synthetic optimization tasks and transfers that knowledge to new problems with very limited, low-quality data.

Results
Up to 1,900%higher normalized objective value than the best design available in the poor-quality offline data.
#1 Overallbest mean rank across six optimization tasks among 10 evaluated methods.

Using only the lowest-performing 1% of historical data, OptBias ranked first on 4 of 6 tasks and second on the remaining two.

My role: Led the project end-to-end, including method design, synthetic task generation, meta-learning implementation, experiments, and analysis.

Innovative Applications of Artificial Intelligence (IAAI), AAAI 2026Deployed AI

Discovery of Feasible 3D Printing Configurations for Metal Alloys via AI-Driven Adaptive Experimental Design

Challenge

Finding workable settings for printing GRCop-42 is a rare-event search problem: among more than 100 million possible configurations at each laser-power level, most settings fail and feasible ones are extremely rare. Each physical experiment is also costly in time and materials.

What We Built

We developed BEAM, an AI-guided experimental design framework that learns from previous successes and failures, estimates where feasible configurations are most likely to exist, and selects the most promising settings for the next physical printing experiments.

Results
37 → 0Manual configurations tested by our collaborators over more than six months without finding a feasible setting.
6 Feasibleconfigurations discovered across all four tested laser-power levels, with no more than 10 AI-guided physical trials per level.

My role: Led the AI/ML work, including problem formulation, probabilistic modeling, search strategy design, experiment planning with materials-science collaborators, and analysis of physical printing results.

AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI) 2027 · Under ReviewWearable Systems

OptWear: AI-Driven Human-Centered Design of Wearable Health Systems

Challenge

Designing wearable health systems requires balancing competing goals such as energy efficiency and latency, while evaluating every candidate on real hardware is expensive and time-consuming.

What We Built

We developed OptWear, a multi-fidelity optimization framework that combines inexpensive simulations with selective hardware evaluations and adapts the search to application-specific energy and latency priorities.

Results
85.2% Lower Costaverage reduction in evaluation cost across five wearable health applications while maintaining comparable design quality.
5 Applicationsoptimized across different energy-latency preferences, enabling the selected design to adapt to application-specific deployment priorities.

My role: Led the AI/ML formulation, surrogate modeling, multi-fidelity optimization, preference-aware search, experiments, and analysis in collaboration with the wearable-systems team.

Ongoing ResearchConstrained Multi-Objective Optimization

Data-Efficient Discovery of PFAS-Capturing Materials via Constrained Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization

Challenge

PFAS material discovery requires identifying designs that simultaneously achieve high uptake and high selectivity while satisfying synthesis feasibility constraints.

What We Built

I developed a feasibility-aware multi-objective Bayesian optimization framework that learns from experimental results and prioritizes candidates with strong performance trade-offs that are also likely to be synthesizable.

Preliminary Results

After five optimization rounds, discovered candidates achieved up to ~3,300% higher uptake and ~150% higher selectivity than the literature baseline, while expanding the set of high-performing trade-offs between both objectives.

My role: Lead the computational optimization, including problem formulation, surrogate and feasibility modeling, candidate selection, and coordination with experimental collaborators.
